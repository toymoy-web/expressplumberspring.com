Water Line Repair & Replacement in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing repairs and replaces water service lines throughout Spring, Texas, the underground pipe running from the meter or curb stop to the house itself. This is a distinct line from the interior plumbing covered by repiping, and it fails for different reasons and gets repaired differently.

Most water line calls start the same way as a slab leak: a water bill that's jumped with no change in usage, or a wet or unusually green patch of yard between the meter and the house that stays damp even in dry weather.

2

High Water Bill or a Wet Spot in the Yard?

A water meter that keeps spinning with every fixture in the house shut off points to a leak somewhere between the meter and the house, not inside it. If that spinning meter pairs with a soft, soggy, or unusually green patch of yard along the likely path of the line, that's a strong sign the leak has surfaced enough to saturate the soil above it. Older galvanized service lines and lines that have shifted with the same expansive clay soil that causes slab leaks are the most common causes we find.

3

How We Diagnose and Locate It

We start with the same meter test used for any suspected leak, shutting off every fixture in the house and watching whether the meter still moves. If it does, the leak is on the service line side, not inside the home.

From there, acoustic and electronic detection equipment pinpoint the exact location along the line's path before we recommend excavation, the same diagnostic-first approach we use for any leak detection call.

Every repair or replacement method follows the IPC's requirements for buried water service lines, including proper depth and material standards for this area.

4

Spot Repair or Full Line Replacement

Spot repair

For a single, isolated failure point on an otherwise sound line, digging just that section is the faster, less disruptive option.

Trenchless replacement

Pulls a new line through with minimal trenching, worth considering for a longer run or a line with multiple failure points.

Open trench replacement

Sometimes the most direct option depending on the line's depth, material, and what's above it in the yard.

Common Questions

How is a water line different from a slab leak?
A slab leak is inside the home's foundation footprint. A water line runs underground between the meter and the house, often through the yard. Both get found with similar detection equipment, but the repair access and methods differ.
Can you replace my water line without tearing up the whole yard?
Often, yes. Trenchless replacement pulls a new line through with a much narrower trench than a full open-cut replacement, and we'll tell you honestly whether your specific situation qualifies.
Who's responsible for the line, me or the water utility?
Generally, the property owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house. The utility typically owns the line up to and including the meter itself. We can help you understand where that boundary sits for your property.
Do you handle the permit for water line work?
Yes. Water line work in unincorporated Harris and Montgomery County typically requires a permit and final inspection, and we pull it as part of the job rather than leaving you to handle it separately.
How long does water line replacement take?
A trenchless replacement is often completed in a single day. Open trench replacement can take longer depending on line length and what's above it. We'll give you a real timeline once we know the actual scope.

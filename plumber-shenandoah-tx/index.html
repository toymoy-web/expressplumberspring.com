A Small City With a Notable History

Spring Express Plumbing dispatches to Shenandoah, a small incorporated city in Montgomery County with under 3,500 residents packed into under 2 square miles. Shenandoah is internationally known as the hometown of David Vetter, known as the "boy in the plastic bubble," and the city renamed Tamina School Road to David Memorial Drive in 1986 in his honor.

Like its immediate neighbors Oak Ridge North and The Woodlands, Shenandoah runs its own municipal water service separate from Spring's, and its housing stock is mostly built out from the 1970s onward.

Shenandoah sits just off I-45, close enough to The Woodlands that the two communities blend together in daily life even though they're governed separately, with Shenandoah's own city council and water system distinct from the Township next door.

A City Known for More Than Its Size

David Vetter lived with severe combined immunodeficiency and spent much of his childhood in a sterile plastic enclosure, becoming a well-known figure in medical history during the late 1970s and early 1980s. Shenandoah's decision to rename a road in his honor in 1986 is a real, documented piece of the city's identity, not a footnote, and it's the kind of local detail longtime residents actually know their city for. Every job we do here still meets the same IPC standard we follow everywhere else in our service area.

Gas Line Installation & Repair in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing installs and repairs gas lines throughout Spring, Texas for water heaters, ranges, dryers, and outdoor fixtures like fire pits and grills. Gas work has a lower margin for error than most plumbing, which is why every job gets pressure-tested before we consider it finished.

The sulfur or rotten-egg smell associated with a gas leak is not natural gas's actual odor. Utilities add a compound called mercaptan specifically so a leak is noticeable by smell, since natural gas itself is odorless. If you smell it, that's the system working as designed to warn you.

Most calls fall into two categories: a new line run for an appliance being added or relocated, and a repair or leak investigation on an existing line, sometimes triggered by that mercaptan smell, sometimes by a failed pressure test during an unrelated inspection.

2

How We Handle Gas Work

For a new run, we size the line correctly for the appliance's BTU demand and the length of run required, since undersized gas lines are a real and common cause of appliances that don't perform at full capacity even though they're technically getting gas.

Every gas line job, new or repaired, gets pressure-tested before we sign off. That means pressurizing the line and confirming it holds steady over time, not just a visual check or a soap-bubble test on a fitting or two.

Work follows the International Fuel Gas Code as adopted and amended by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, covering line sizing, material, and required shutoff valve placement for every appliance connection.

Common Gas Line Jobs

New appliance line run

Sizing and running a new line for a water heater, range, dryer, or outdoor fixture being added or relocated.

Leak repair

Locating and repairing the specific point of a leak found through smell, a failed pressure test, or a utility company flag.

Line replacement

For older galvanized gas lines that have corroded internally, a full section or line replacement rather than a series of spot repairs.

✓

If You Smell Gas Right Now

Do not turn any light switches, appliances, or anything electrical on or off.
Do not light a match, use a lighter, or create any open flame.
Leave the house and get a safe distance away before calling anyone.
Call your gas utility's emergency line first, then call us for the repair once the utility has secured the line.

Common Questions

How do I know if I have a gas leak?
The sulfur or rotten-egg smell from added mercaptan is the most reliable sign. A hissing sound near a gas appliance or line, or dead vegetation over a buried line, are other signs. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your utility's emergency line immediately.
Do you install gas lines for outdoor fire pits and grills?
Yes, including proper line sizing and burial depth for the run from the house to the fixture, with the required shutoff valve installed.
How long does a new gas line installation take?
A single appliance line run typically takes half a day including the pressure test. Longer runs or multiple appliances take longer, and we'll give you a real estimate once we see the scope.
Can you convert my house from propane to natural gas, or add a line to a propane tank?
Yes, both. Conversion or new propane line work follows the same sizing and pressure-testing standards as natural gas, with a few material differences we'll walk you through for your specific setup.

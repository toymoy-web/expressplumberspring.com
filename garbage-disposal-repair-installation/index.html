Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing repairs and installs garbage disposals throughout Spring, Texas. A disposal that hums but doesn't spin is one of the most common calls we get, and it's also one of the fastest and least expensive to actually fix.

That humming sound almost always means the motor is getting power but the flywheel is jammed, usually by a small object like a fruit pit or bone fragment that got past the baffle. A disposal leaking from the bottom, by contrast, usually means the unit's internal seal has failed and needs replacement rather than repair.

A disposal that trips the breaker repeatedly, rather than just humming, is a different problem. That usually points to a motor drawing too much current, either from a jam that's been running too long or a genuine motor failure, and it's worth having checked before repeatedly resetting the breaker.

2

What We Check

For a jammed unit, we manually free the flywheel using the reset button and, if needed, the hex key opening on the bottom of most units, then check for whatever caused the jam in the first place.

For a leak, we identify whether it's coming from the sink flange seal at the top, the internal unit body itself, or the dishwasher and drain line connections, since each has a different fix and only a body leak actually requires full replacement.

For a unit that won't reset or keeps tripping the breaker, we test the motor directly rather than assuming it's dead, since sometimes the issue is upstream in the electrical connection rather than the disposal itself.

3

Repair or Replace

Jam clearing and reset

The fast, inexpensive fix for a unit that hums but won't spin, usually resolved in the same visit.

Seal or connection repair

For a leak traced to the flange or a drain connection rather than the unit body itself.

Full disposal replacement

Necessary for a body leak, a burned-out motor, or a unit old enough that parts are no longer worth chasing.

✓

Simple Habits That Prevent Most Disposal Jams

Run cold water, not hot, while the disposal is on. Cold keeps grease solid so it grinds instead of gumming up the blades.
Avoid fibrous material like celery, corn husks, and onion skins, which tend to wrap around the flywheel instead of grinding cleanly.
Never put bones, fruit pits, or glass down a disposal, the single most common cause of a jam.
Run the disposal regularly, even briefly, so parts don't seize from disuse between uses.

Common Questions

My disposal hums but won't turn. Is that expensive to fix?
Usually not. That's almost always a jammed flywheel, and clearing it is one of the fastest, least expensive calls we handle. Most jams are resolved in well under an hour.
Why is my garbage disposal leaking?
Depends on where. A leak at the sink flange is a seal issue, fixable without replacing the unit. A leak from the body of the unit itself means the internal seal has failed, and that means replacement, not repair.
How long does disposal replacement take?
A standard swap with the same mounting style takes under an hour. If the new unit uses a different mounting system than the old one, plan on a bit longer.

A Small City With Its Own Water District

Spring Express Plumbing serves Oak Ridge North, a small incorporated city of about 3,000 residents packed into just 1.45 square miles. Residents voted to incorporate on January 20, 1979, specifically to control their own Municipal Utility District and avoid being annexed by Houston, and that MUD-based water system is still how the city operates today.

Because Oak Ridge North built out almost entirely in a fairly narrow window starting in the late 1970s, the housing stock here is more consistent in age than in a larger city with decades of mixed development. That actually makes some things easier to predict.

The city's small size hasn't stopped it from investing in real infrastructure and community spaces, Teddy Bear Park and Marilyn Edgar Park both serve residents within a city limit you could drive across in a few minutes. That same compact footprint means dispatch times to Oak Ridge North tend to run efficiently once a call comes in.

What a Compact, Consistent City Means for Plumbing

Homes built in the same general era tend to hit the same failure points around the same time. That means aging water heaters, original supply lines reaching the end of their service life, and the first wave of slab leaks in homes now approaching or past the half-century mark. We see this pattern repeat across Oak Ridge North's small footprint more consistently than in cities with a wider mix of construction decades. At just 1.45 square miles with around 3,000 residents, it's one of the smallest cities in our service area, which in practice means we're rarely far from a call once it comes in.

Incorporated to Stay Independent

The 1979 vote to incorporate wasn't just paperwork, it was a deliberate choice by residents of the old Oak Ridge Municipal Utility District to keep control of their own water infrastructure and tax rate rather than be absorbed into Houston. That independent streak still shows in how the city runs its utilities today, and it's part of why we treat Oak Ridge North as its own place, not an extension of a neighboring city. Every repair here still follows the same IPC requirements enforced by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners as anywhere else we work.

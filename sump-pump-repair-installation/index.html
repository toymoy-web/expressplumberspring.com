Sump Pump Repair & Installation in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing repairs and installs sump pumps across Spring, Klein, Tomball, and the surrounding communities. A sump pump sits in a pit at the lowest point of a crawlspace, drainage system, or below-grade area and moves water out before it can pool. When the pump fails, the pit fills, and the water has nowhere to go but into the structure.

Most homes in this area are built on slabs with no basement, so a sump pump is not standard equipment here the way it is in colder states. That is exactly why it is worth being direct. A pump manages water that reaches a pit. It does not stop water from entering a yard, and it is not a flood barrier. We look at where the water comes from before recommending anything.

Or a detached garage or workshop that sits lower than the lot. Each one calls for a different pump and a different discharge plan.

4

How We Diagnose a Pump That Is Not Working

We start with power. A tripped GFCI outlet or breaker is a common reason a pump seems dead. Next we check the float switch, which can stick or tangle, then the intake screen, then the check valve that keeps water from draining back into the pit, and finally the motor itself.

We test by filling the pit slowly until the float lifts, then watch for the discharge to run clear and the pit to empty. A pump that runs constantly, short-cycles, or runs without moving water usually has a float, check valve, or discharge line problem rather than a dead motor. Installations follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Texas.

5

Repair, Replace, or Add Backup

Repair

A stuck float, a failed check valve, or a clogged intake can often be fixed without replacing the pump, especially on a newer unit.

Replace

Pumps wear out, often somewhere around 7 to 10 years depending on how hard they work. A failing older pump usually costs less to replace than to keep repairing.

Battery backup

Storms cause power outages when a pump is needed most. A battery backup keeps the pump running through an outage.

✓

Emergency Steps if Water Is Rising

Stay out of standing water near outlets, extension cords, and electrical panels. Water and electricity together are the real danger.
If you can reach the breaker from dry ground, turn off power to the pump circuit and any affected outlets.
Move stored boxes and belongings off the floor of a crawlspace or garage to higher ground if it is safe to do so.
Photograph the water level and the damage before cleanup for your insurance claim.
Call us right away so we can plan the repair, and ask your insurance company what it requires before cleanup starts.

Common Questions

Do I need a sump pump in Spring, TX?
Most homes here do not, because typical Houston-area homes sit on slabs with no basement. A pump makes sense for a crawlspace, a drainage pit, or a below-grade space that collects water. We check where the water is coming from before recommending one.
How long does a sump pump last?
Often around 7 to 10 years, depending on how often it runs and how well it is maintained. A pump that runs constantly during wet seasons tends to wear out sooner.
Why does my sump pump run constantly?
A stuck float, a failed check valve, or a high water table can all keep a pump running. The check valve is a frequent culprit because it lets water drain back into the pit after each cycle.
Will my sump pump work during a power outage?
Not unless it has a battery backup or another backup power source. Storms often knock out power exactly when a pump is needed, which is why backups matter.
Does homeowners insurance cover sump pump failure?
Standard policies often exclude flood and groundwater seepage, and sump pump failure coverage is usually a separate endorsement. Check your policy or ask your agent before assuming either way.
Is a failed sump or ejector pump an emergency?
During rain or with a bathroom draining into an ejector pit, yes. A pump that stops can flood the area within hours, so call the 24/7 line and we send a plumber.

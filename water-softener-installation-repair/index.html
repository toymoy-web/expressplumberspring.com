Water Softener Installation & Repair in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing installs and repairs water softeners throughout Spring, Texas. A softener specifically addresses water hardness, dissolved calcium and magnesium, through an ion exchange process using salt, which is a different job than the carbon filtration covered on our water filtration page.

Most softener calls fall into two groups: a new install for a home that's never had one, or a repair on an existing unit that's stopped softening properly even though it's still running.

A softener that's failing usually shows up gradually. Spots return to dishes and shower glass, soap stops lathering the way it used to, and laundry starts feeling stiffer even though nothing else in the house changed.

A softener that's simply been neglected, no salt added in months, is one of the most common calls we get and one of the least expensive to sort out. Before assuming the unit itself has failed, checking the salt level is always the first move.

2

Diagnosing an Existing Softener

We check the brine tank for salt bridging, a hardened crust that stops salt from dissolving into the brine solution the unit needs to regenerate its resin bed. This is one of the most common reasons a softener quietly stops working while still appearing to run normally.

We also test the resin bed's actual performance and check the bypass valve and control head, since a softener stuck in bypass mode will run water straight past the softening media without you necessarily noticing right away.

If the unit has an electronic control head, we check the programmed regeneration schedule too. A schedule that no longer matches your household's actual water use is a quieter cause of poor performance than an outright mechanical failure.

Any new install ties into the main line per IPC backflow and cross-connection standards, the same requirement covered on our water filtration page for systems that tie into whole-home plumbing.

3

Repair, Resin Replacement, or New Install

Salt bridge clearing and recalibration

Often resolves a softener that's stopped working with no parts needed, just clearing the tank and resetting the regeneration cycle.

Resin bed replacement

Worth it when the resin itself has degraded, usually after 10-15 years of service, rather than the control head or valves.

New system install

Sized to your household's actual water usage and hardness level, not a one-size package pulled off a shelf.

Common Questions

How do I know if I need a new softener or just a repair?
If the unit is under 10 years old and the resin bed tests fine, it's almost always a repair, often something as simple as clearing a salt bridge. Past 10-15 years with degraded resin, replacement usually makes more sense.
How much salt does a water softener actually need?
It depends on household size and water hardness, but most residential units need a bag or two of salt added every four to eight weeks. We'll give you the real number for your specific unit and usage.
Can hard water actually damage my plumbing?
Over years, yes. Scale buildup from hard water narrows pipe diameter gradually and shortens the life of water heaters and fixtures. A softener is as much a protective measure for your plumbing as a comfort upgrade.
What type of salt should I use in my softener?
Pelletized or crystal water softener salt, not rock salt, which dissolves less evenly and leaves more residue in the brine tank over time.

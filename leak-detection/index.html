Leak Detection in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing locates hidden leaks throughout Spring, Texas using electronic and acoustic detection equipment, rather than guessing based on where water happens to be showing up.

Spring sits on the same expansive clay soil found across the Gulf Coast, which swells when wet and shrinks during dry stretches. That movement stresses the copper and cast iron lines running under an older slab foundation, and it is the single most common cause of the slab leaks we get called out for.

Emergency Leak Detection: When to Call Right Now

A slow leak can wait for an appointment, but some cannot. Water coming through a ceiling, a meter spinning fast with everything off, or a soaked floor with no obvious source all need a plumber today.

Our 24/7 line takes these calls around the clock. We start at the meter, isolate the line, and find the source before we recommend any repair.

High Water Bill or a Sound You Can't Place?

A water bill that jumps with no change in usage, a water meter that keeps spinning with every fixture in the house shut off, or a warm patch on the floor with no visible water anywhere. These are the classic signs of a leak you cannot see, most often a slab leak or a service line leak between the meter and the house.

The leak does not have to be dramatic to be real. A pinhole leak under a slab can run for weeks before enough water surfaces to notice.

4

How We Find It, Even on an Emergency Call

We start at the meter. Shutting off every fixture in the house and watching whether the meter still moves tells us within minutes whether there is a leak at all, and roughly how large it is.

From there we use acoustic listening equipment to pinpoint sound traveling through a pressurized line, and electronic leak detection gear for slab and underground lines where sound alone is not precise enough. We mark the exact location before recommending any excavation or slab work, not an approximate area.

Any repair we recommend follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted and amended by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, not a shortcut method chosen to save a return trip.

5

What Happens After We Find It

Spot repair

When the leak is isolated to one section of pipe, a targeted repair at that exact point is usually faster and less invasive than it sounds.

Reroute

For a slab leak, rerouting the line through the attic or a wall cavity instead of breaking concrete is often the less disruptive option, and we will lay out both paths honestly.

Repipe evaluation

If detection turns up more than one leak, or the pipe material itself is clearly failing, that points toward a broader repiping conversation rather than repeated spot repairs.

6

Leaks Behind Walls, Under Floors and Through Ceilings

Many of our leak calls start with a stain, not a drip. A brown patch on a ceiling below a bathroom, a damp spot on a garage wall, water seeping across a kitchen floor, or a soft spot near a toilet. The wet spot is rarely the leak itself.

We trace the water back to its source before anyone opens a wall. If your home has a crawlspace or an attic run, we check those paths too, then tell you what we found and what a repair involves.

7

Irrigation, Yard and Meter-Box Leaks

Outdoor leaks hide well. A sprinkler zone that runs weak, a soggy patch of lawn near the meter box, a green stripe of grass over a buried line, or a wet spot near a hot tub or pool equipment.

We isolate zones and lines one at a time to narrow the search. Pool and spa work here means the supply plumbing and valves feeding the equipment, not the pool shell itself.

Common Questions

How do you find a leak without tearing up my floor?
Acoustic and electronic leak detection equipment lets us pinpoint the source from sound and signal readings, usually within a foot or two, before any concrete or flooring is disturbed. We mark the spot and explain what we found before recommending next steps.
Why do so many Spring homes get slab leaks?
Spring sits on expansive Gulf Coast clay soil that swells and shrinks with moisture changes, which stresses the copper and cast iron lines running under an older slab foundation. Homes built before the mid-1990s are where we see this most.
My water bill doubled but I don't see any water. Could it still be a leak?
Yes, and it's one of the most common ways we find slab leaks. A steady underground or under-slab leak can run for weeks without ever surfacing visibly, showing up only on the meter and the bill.
How long does a leak detection visit take?
Most visits run one to two hours, starting with the meter test and moving to acoustic or electronic equipment if the leak location isn't obvious from there. We tell you what we found and the likely repair path before we leave, not days later.
What does leak detection cost?
It depends on how hard the leak is to reach and how many lines we have to isolate. A leak near a fixture costs far less to find than one under a slab. Call and describe what you see, and we quote the actual job.
Will you fix the leak once you find it?
Yes. We find the source and then repair it, so you do not need to hire a second company. If the repair is a bigger job, we explain the options and price before starting.
Do you find leaks in rentals and small multifamily buildings?
Yes. Tell us the unit count and where the water shows up, and we plan the visit around isolating each line. Multi-unit leaks often need more than one test to rule out each unit.

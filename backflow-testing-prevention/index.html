Backflow Testing & Prevention in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing tests and repairs backflow prevention assemblies throughout Spring, Texas, most commonly on irrigation systems and commercial water connections where local water providers require annual certified testing.

Backflow prevention exists to stop water from flowing the wrong direction through a connection, back into the clean municipal supply, which can happen when pressure drops unexpectedly and a connected system like an irrigation line or a commercial process pulls water backward instead of forward.

The device itself is a mechanical assembly with check valves and a relief mechanism, installed at the point where your irrigation or commercial line connects to the municipal supply. Like any mechanical device, the internal components wear over time, which is exactly why annual testing exists rather than a one-time install-and-forget approach.

2

Got a Notice That Your Backflow Test Is Due?

Many water providers in the Spring area, and Texas generally, require an annual test on backflow prevention assemblies for irrigation systems, commercial properties, and certain other connections, with results reported back to the provider. If you received a renewal notice or a letter about an overdue test, that's exactly what this service covers, a certified test with the paperwork filed on your behalf, not a guess about what the notice means.

3

What the Test Involves

We test the assembly under controlled conditions to confirm both check valves and the relief valve are functioning within spec, using calibrated test equipment rather than a visual inspection alone.

If the device fails, we can typically repair or rebuild it on the spot with common replacement parts. A device beyond repair gets replaced, and we retest before filing results with your water provider either way.

4

Testing, Repair, or New Installation

Annual certified test

The standard yearly requirement for most irrigation and commercial backflow assemblies, with results filed on your behalf.

Repair or rebuild

For a device that fails testing but is otherwise sound, replacing the internal components is usually faster and cheaper than a new assembly.

New assembly installation

Required when adding a new irrigation system or commercial connection, or replacing a device too far gone to rebuild.

✓

What to Have Ready Before Your Test

Clear access to the backflow assembly, usually located near the water meter or irrigation valve box.
Have your renewal notice or account number handy if your water provider sent one.
Know the assembly's approximate age if this is the first test since installation.
If it's an irrigation system, run a quick check that zones are functioning before the visit, this helps separate irrigation issues from backflow device issues.

Common Questions

Do I really need to test my backflow preventer every year?
If your water provider requires it, which is common for irrigation systems and commercial connections in this area, yes. Skipping it can result in a notice or, eventually, water service action from the provider.
What happens if my backflow device fails the test?
We can usually repair or rebuild it the same visit with common parts. If it's too degraded to repair, we'll quote a replacement, then retest and file the passing result with your provider.
Do you handle the paperwork with the water utility?
Yes, filing test results with your specific water provider is part of the service, not something we leave for you to submit separately.
What's the difference between a double check valve and an RPZ assembly?
A double check valve suits lower-hazard connections like most residential irrigation. An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone assembly, is required for higher-hazard commercial connections. We'll confirm which your property needs if you're unsure.

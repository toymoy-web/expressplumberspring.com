Frozen Pipe Repair in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing repairs frozen and burst pipes across Spring, Texas during winter freeze events. Texas homes, including most in Spring, are not built with the same pipe insulation standards as homes in consistently cold climates, because hard freezes here are genuinely rare, which is exactly why they cause so much damage when they happen.

February 2021's Winter Storm Uri is the clearest example most Spring homeowners remember. Attic lines, exterior-wall runs, and hose bibs across the Houston area froze and, in many cases, split, because they were never built to handle sustained freezing temperatures the way pipes in northern states are.

2

What Freezes First, and Why

Pipes running through an unconditioned attic or along an exterior wall are the most exposed, since they have the least protection from a home's heated interior air. A hose bib or an exposed line in a garage is also high-risk, sitting largely outside the building envelope's warmth.

A pipe does not have to visibly burst to cause damage. Ice expanding inside a closed line builds pressure that can crack a fitting or a straight section that doesn't show water until it thaws, sometimes a day or more after the freeze itself has passed.

3

During and After a Freeze

Active freeze response

If a line is frozen but not yet split, careful, controlled thawing can prevent a break before it happens. Never use an open flame.

Split pipe repair

Once thawed, a split section is repaired the same way as any burst pipe, cut out and replaced or fitted, depending on location and access.

Prevention for next time

Pipe insulation, heat tape on the most exposed runs, and knowing where your main shutoff is before the next freeze warning.

✓

What to Do Before the Next Freeze Warning

Disconnect garden hoses and drain outdoor hose bibs completely.
Locate your main water shutoff now, not during an active freeze when you're in a hurry.
Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air can reach the pipes inside.
Let faucets drip slightly on the coldest nights, moving water is less likely to freeze solid.
Know which lines in your home run through the attic or an exterior wall, the highest-risk spots.

Common Questions

How do I know if a pipe is frozen versus just not working?
No water at all from one fixture while others work normally, especially after a hard freeze warning, points to a frozen line. Frost or unusual cold on an exposed pipe section is another sign. Don't wait to find out, shut off the main if you suspect a freeze and call.
Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?
Carefully, with a hair dryer or warm towels on an exposed section, yes. Never use an open flame or anything that could ignite nearby insulation or wood framing. If you can't reach the frozen section safely, call rather than improvise.
How can I protect my pipes before the next freeze?
Insulate exposed lines in the attic, garage, and exterior walls, disconnect and drain hose bibs, and know where your main shutoff is. We can walk your specific home and point out the highest-risk spots before the next cold snap.
Does homeowners insurance cover frozen pipe damage?
Often yes for the resulting water damage, since a freeze is considered a sudden, accidental event. Ask your adjuster about your specific policy rather than assuming either way.

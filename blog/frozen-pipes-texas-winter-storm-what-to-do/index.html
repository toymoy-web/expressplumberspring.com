Frozen Pipes in a Texas Winter Storm: What to Do

By the Spring Express Plumbing Team · Published 2026-09-15 · Updated 2026-09-22

If a faucet produces no water or just a trickle during a hard freeze, you likely have a frozen pipe. Shut off the fixture, locate your home's main water valve in case the pipe has already cracked, and apply gentle heat, a hair dryer or warm towels, to the exposed section of pipe starting near the faucet and working back. Never use an open flame. If water is already leaking or spraying, skip the thawing step, shut off the main valve immediately, and call a plumber.

Why Texas Homes Freeze Differently Than Northern Homes

Homes in consistently cold climates are built with pipe insulation and interior routing designed around regular freezes. Texas homes, including most in Spring, are not, because hard freezes here are genuinely rare. That's exactly why they cause so much damage when they happen.

Winter Storm Uri in February 2021 is the clearest example. According to Texas Farm Bureau Insurance, roughly 16 percent of Texans reported pipes freezing and bursting during that storm, with an average insurance claim of about $27,000. Attic lines, exterior-wall runs, and hose bibs across the state froze because they were never built to handle sustained freezing temperatures.

What to Do While a Pipe Is Frozen

Shut off the affected fixture so you're not left with an open tap once flow resumes unexpectedly.

Apply gentle heat with a hair dryer, heating pad, or warm towels, starting at the faucet end and working toward the blockage.

Never use an open flame or a high-heat device that could ignite nearby insulation or framing.

If you can't reach the frozen section safely, or thawing isn't restoring flow, stop and call a plumber rather than continuing to apply heat blindly.

If a Pipe Has Already Burst

Shut off your home's main water valve immediately. This is the single most damage-limiting action you can take, and it's worth knowing where that valve is before a freeze warning, not during one.

Shut off electricity to any area where water is contacting outlets or fixtures, if you can do so safely. Then call for frozen pipe repair or burst pipe repair, and document the damage with photos for insurance before cleanup starts.

Preventing It Before the Next Freeze

Disconnect and drain garden hoses completely, since a hose left attached traps water against the spigot. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air can reach the pipes inside. Let faucets drip slightly on the coldest nights, since moving water resists freezing better than still water.

Knowing where your main shutoff valve is, and confirming everyone in the household knows too, is the single piece of preparation that matters most when a freeze warning actually arrives. Pipe insulation sleeves on any exposed lines in a garage, attic, or crawlspace add another real layer of protection for very little cost, and they're worth installing well before the first freeze warning of the season rather than scrambling once one is issued.

Which Pipes Are Actually at Risk in a Spring, TX Home

Not every pipe in a Texas home faces the same freeze risk. The most vulnerable lines are the ones that were never expected to need protection: exterior hose bibs, pipes running through an uninsulated attic, and any supply line routed along an exterior wall, especially in homes built before more recent energy codes required better wall insulation.

Homes in Spring's older sections, including parts of Old Town Spring with plumbing dating back decades, often have less wall insulation around these runs than newer construction in subdivisions built over the last ten to fifteen years. That doesn't mean newer homes are immune, an attic line is an attic line regardless of the home's age. It does mean older homes deserve a more thorough check before the first hard freeze of the season, including areas that might not seem obviously exposed at first glance.

The Cost of Waiting Versus the Cost of Prevention

The prevention steps in this post cost close to nothing: a few dollars for pipe insulation sleeves, an afternoon disconnecting hoses and checking exterior walls. The cost of a burst pipe, by contrast, is rarely just the plumbing repair itself. Water spreading through drywall, flooring, and insulation before anyone notices often means the water damage cleanup costs more than the original pipe repair.

That gap is exactly why Winter Storm Uri's average claim figure ran as high as it did. Most of that $27,000 average wasn't the cost of fixing a cracked pipe, it was the water damage that pipe caused while it went unnoticed or unaddressed. A few hours of prevention before a freeze warning is a genuinely small investment against that kind of outcome. It's the kind of thing that's easy to put off right up until the week a hard freeze actually arrives, by which point it's already too late to insulate anything.

It's worth treating any hard freeze warning in this area as a genuine event rather than routine weather, precisely because Texas infrastructure, both municipal and residential, simply isn't built with the same freeze tolerance as homes further north. A forecast that would barely register in a colder state deserves real preparation here.

Common Questions

How long does it take for a pipe to freeze in Texas?
It depends on the pipe's location and exposure, but an exterior-wall or attic line can freeze within several hours of sustained temperatures well below freezing. That's faster than most homeowners expect given how rarely it happens here and how little most Texas homes are built to resist it, unlike homes in consistently colder climates.
Should I leave my faucets dripping during a freeze warning?
Yes, a slow drip on both hot and cold lines, especially for fixtures on exterior walls, keeps water moving and reduces the chance of a full freeze.
Is frozen pipe damage covered by homeowners insurance?
Often yes for the resulting water damage, since a freeze is considered a sudden, accidental event. Check your specific policy or ask your adjuster rather than assuming, since some policies do carry exclusions for damage tied to inadequate heating or a vacant property.
Can I thaw a pipe with a space heater?
Carefully, and only if it's positioned safely away from anything flammable and you can monitor it continuously. A hair dryer is generally safer for direct, controlled heat on an exposed pipe section.
How many pipes usually freeze at once during a Texas cold snap?
It varies by home, but exterior-facing fixtures and any attic or exterior-wall runs tend to freeze together, since they're all exposed to the same drop in temperature. That's part of why a single freeze event can mean multiple repair points in one house, not just one, which is also why plumbers see call volume spike so sharply during and right after a hard freeze.
Can a pipe burst even if it doesn't fully freeze?
Yes. Partial ice blockages can still build enough pressure to crack a pipe, especially at a weak joint or fitting, even if water is still trickling through elsewhere in the line, which is why low flow alone shouldn't be treated as a minor issue.

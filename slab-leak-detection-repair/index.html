Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing detects and repairs slab leaks throughout Spring, Texas. A slab leak is specifically a leak in a water line running underneath a home's concrete foundation, distinct from a leak in an accessible wall or attic line, and it needs its own diagnostic approach and repair methods.

Once a slab leak is located precisely, usually with the acoustic and electronic equipment covered on our leak detection page, the real decision is what to do about it. That decision is what this page focuses on.

2

Why Spring Homes Get Slab Leaks

Spring sits on expansive Gulf Coast clay soil that swells when saturated and shrinks during dry spells. That constant movement stresses copper and cast iron lines running under an older slab, eventually wearing through at a joint or a point of contact with the concrete or rebar.

Homes built before the mid-1990s, when copper was the standard supply line material, are where we see this most. Newer homes with PEX supply lines are less prone to this specific failure mode, though not immune to a slab leak from a different cause.

A slab leak left alone does not fix itself. Left long enough, the water can undermine soil support under the foundation itself, which is a much larger and more expensive problem than the original plumbing repair would have been.

3

Reroute, Spot Repair, or Repipe

Reroute

Runs a new line through the attic or wall cavities instead of breaking concrete, often the least disruptive option for a single slab leak.

Spot repair

Breaking concrete at the exact leak location to repair that specific section, worth considering when the rest of the line is sound.

Full repipe evaluation

If detection turns up more than one leak or the pipe material is clearly failing broadly, repeated spot repairs stop making sense compared to a full repipe.

✓

What to Watch For Between Now and Repair Day

Note whether the warm spot or sound has changed size or intensity since you first noticed it.
Keep an eye on your water meter with everything off. A moving dial confirms the leak is still active.
Avoid placing rugs or furniture directly over the suspected leak area until it's repaired.
Photograph any visible flooring damage now for your insurance file, before repair work begins.

Common Questions

How do I know if I have a slab leak?
A warm spot on the floor with no visible water, a water bill that jumps with no change in usage, or the sound of running water when everything is off are the classic signs. A detection visit confirms it and pinpoints the exact location.
Is a reroute better than breaking concrete for a slab leak?
Often, but not always. A reroute avoids disturbing the slab entirely, which is usually less disruptive and faster. Whether it's the right call depends on the line's layout and where the leak sits, which we assess before recommending either path.
Will my homeowners insurance cover a slab leak repair?
Often the resulting water damage is covered, but the plumbing repair itself frequently is not, or is subject to a specific slab leak endorsement. Check your specific policy or ask your adjuster before assuming either way.
How long does slab leak repair take?
A reroute or single spot repair is typically completed in one day. Multiple leak points or a decision to move toward a full repipe extends the timeline, and we'll walk you through the real schedule once detection is complete.

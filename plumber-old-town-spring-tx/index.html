Old Town Spring Is a Business District First

Old Town Spring is the original town of Spring, Texas, centered on the crossing of Spring-Cypress Road and Hardy Road in unincorporated Harris County. It is known mainly as a shopping and dining destination, with more than 150 shops, restaurants, and galleries, many in restored buildings.

We give it its own page because plumbing for a business district differs from plumbing for a home subdivision. If you live in a house near the district, the same dispatch line covers you, and our Spring page covers the wider area.

Where Old Town Spring Came From

The settlement began as Camp Spring, platted by the International and Great Northern Railroad in 1873. By 1901 it was a crossroads for two rail lines. Merchants began moving in around the Spring Cafe in the late 1960s, the Old Town Spring Association opened in 1980, and the area was a tourist destination by 1989.

That history matters for plumbing in one practical way. Some buildings in the district are more than 100 years old, and others have been remodeled for shops and restaurants. We make no assumptions about what is behind a wall. We look at the actual pipes and fixtures first and explain what we find.

Restaurants, Shops, and Event Weekends

Kitchens and restrooms carry the heaviest plumbing load in any shopping district. Restaurants depend on grease traps, floor drains, and prep sinks that cannot stay broken during service. A slow drain or a backed-up floor drain on a Saturday costs a business customers.

The district hosts festivals through the year, including the Texas Crawfish and Music Festival each April. Crowds put extra demand on restrooms and drains, so a repair that waits until Monday can leave a restroom closed. Call the moment something backs up, and tell the dispatcher your business hours.

Businesses with irrigation or similar connections may also need periodic backflow testing. Our backflow testing page explains what that involves.

Common Questions

Do you service businesses in Old Town Spring or only homes?
Both. Commercial calls such as drains, restrooms, water heaters, and leaks come through the same dispatch line as home calls. Our commercial plumbing page explains what we take on.
Is Old Town Spring a neighborhood or a business district?
It is mainly a commercial and tourist district, with homes nearby. We describe it that way on purpose, so you know our service here covers storefronts as well as surrounding residences.
Can you work around a restaurant's service hours?
Tell the dispatcher your hours when you call. Emergencies are handled right away, and planned work such as a water heater replacement can be scheduled around your service times.
Do older buildings need special plumbing work?
Not automatically. A building's age alone does not tell us what is inside the walls. We inspect the actual lines and fixtures, explain what we find, and quote the repair before we begin.
Which ZIP code covers Old Town Spring?
Old Town Spring uses the Spring ZIP code 77373. Nearby streets can carry other Spring ZIP codes, so read the street address to the dispatcher and we will confirm coverage.
What should I do if sewage backs up in a shop restroom?
Stop using the fixtures, keep customers out of the area, and call. We find whether the blockage is in the fixture or the main line, and we explain which before any work begins.

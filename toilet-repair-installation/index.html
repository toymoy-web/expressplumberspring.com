Toilet Repair & Installation in Spring, TX

Spring Express Plumbing repairs and installs toilets throughout Spring, Texas, from a simple running toilet wasting water on the monthly bill to a full replacement when a unit is cracked or outdated.

A toilet that rocks slightly or leaks at the base almost always means the wax ring seal has failed, not the toilet itself. That is a common issue in older Old Town Spring homes where the toilet has never been reset since the original installation.

A toilet manufactured before the mid-1990s can use close to twice the water per flush of a current WaterSense-labeled model. On a household that flushes several times a day, that difference shows up on the water bill, and it's part of why we bring up replacement even on toilets that are not actively broken.

Emergency Toilet Repair: Overflow, Backup or a Leak at the Base

A toilet that overflows, will not flush, or leaks around the base cannot wait. Water on the floor soaks subfloor quickly, and a backup may point past the toilet to the line.

Our 24/7 dispatch handles these calls at any hour. We stop the water, clear or repair the toilet, and check whether the problem is the toilet or the drain behind it.

Stop the overflow

  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • Lift the tank lid and hold the flapper closed if water keeps rising.
  • Put towels down and keep the bathroom door closed.
  • Call the 24/7 line if other drains are slow too.

3

What We Check First

For a running toilet, we check the flapper, the fill valve, and the flush valve seat, since a running toilet is almost always one of those three parts rather than the tank or bowl itself.

For a toilet that rocks or shows water at the base, we pull the toilet to inspect the wax ring and the flange underneath. An older or corroded flange sometimes needs a repair collar before a new toilet or wax ring will seal properly.

We also check the supply line and shutoff valve while the toilet is disconnected. A brittle old supply line is a common source of a slow leak that gets blamed on the toilet itself, and replacing it during the same visit is far cheaper than a separate call later.

4

Repair or Replace

Repair the running or leaking toilet

The right call when the bowl and tank are in good shape and the issue is a worn internal part or a failed wax ring seal.

Full toilet replacement

Worth it for a cracked tank or bowl, a toilet older than 20 years using far more water per flush than a current model, or repeated flange issues that point to a bigger underlying problem.

Comfort-height or accessible model

A taller comfort-height bowl is worth considering for anyone with mobility concerns, and it's a straightforward swap at the same time as a standard replacement.

5

Running, Leaking and Wobbly Toilets

Not every toilet call is urgent, but every one costs water. A toilet that runs on its own, a tank that refills at odd times, or a base that rocks and leaks usually traces to a worn flapper, a fill valve, or a failed wax ring.

We replace the part that failed, reset the toilet on a new wax ring when needed, and test it before we leave. If the toilet is old enough that repairs keep stacking up, we say so and give you a replacement price.

Common Questions

Why does my toilet keep running after I flush?
Almost always a worn flapper not sealing fully, or a fill valve not shutting off at the right water level. Both are inexpensive parts to replace and usually fixed in one visit.
Is it normal for a toilet to rock slightly?
No. A toilet that rocks even slightly usually means the wax ring seal underneath has broken or the flange has corroded, which lets water leak at the base over time and can damage the subfloor if left alone.
How long does a toilet replacement take?
A standard replacement with no flange issues takes under an hour. If the flange needs repair first, plan on a bit longer, and we will tell you on site if that is the case.
Will a new toilet actually lower my water bill?
If the one you're replacing predates the mid-1990s, usually yes and noticeably. Current WaterSense-labeled toilets use meaningfully less water per flush than older models, and that adds up across a household that flushes multiple times a day.
Is a running toilet an emergency?
Not by itself, but it wastes water around the clock and can run up your bill. If water is overflowing, backing up, or leaking onto the floor, treat it as an emergency and call the 24/7 line.
